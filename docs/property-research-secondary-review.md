# Secondary property research review

Reviewed 30 September 2026. This is an editorial review of the 53 finite property references extracted from the family workbook. Three GPT-6 Luna researchers handled bounded groups; the main agent researched six additional accounts, reviewed the evidence, corrected source identity issues and integrated the results.

## Scope and findings

The broader search covered contemporary directories, tithe schedules and maps, local histories, archaeology reports, statutory heritage records, parliamentary records, estate/operator sites and archive catalogues. Research is compiled into the existing authenticated property pages; no research automation or database workflow was added.

- **Coldham:** an 1879 directory independently names Lord Overstone as principal landowner. The account now includes earlier estate history, archaeological context and a modern agricultural sale account. The precise Loyd residence remains unidentified.
- **Withybrook:** the 1912 directory names Richard Loyd as lord of the manor and principal landowner, while naming different occupants at Withybrook Hall and Overstone Lodge. Medieval settlement history and the 1918 sale catalogue are distinguished from later family residence.
- **Welsh holdings:** tithe evidence and local histories distinguish Cilycwm Court Henry, Blaenos and separately named Garth holdings. Similar names alone do not establish continuity with the workbook property.
- **Ballogie:** the Victorian predecessor was replaced in 1982–83. The present photographed house is not described as the surviving nineteenth-century mansion. The wider family workbook also records John Wilmot Warner’s 1941 inheritance and name change to Nicol, as well as estate management; that inheritance awaits independent probate/title corroboration.
- **Threepwood:** the Hall and Hall Farmhouse have distinct heritage entries. Different workbook sheets call Michael’s residence both Hall and Farmhouse, so neither listed building is selected as a proven match.
- **London residences:** Lothbury gains the bank's institutional history and specific archive references; Carlton Gardens gains a documented restoration proposal and a clearly identified discrepancy in Kitchener's occupancy dates. Ovington remains distinct from the Hampshire namesake.
- **Smaller residences:** directory, probate and local-history findings were added where available. Bread Street, Portland Place, Cherry Tree Farm and several other exact house identities remain unresolved.

## Evidence standards

Each historical paragraph and timeline entry links to its sources. Workbook statements remain visibly separate from external corroboration. A catalogue establishes that a document exists, not that its contents have been read. Proposed building work is not described as completed work; an advertised sale is not proof of a completed transfer. Current registered ownership is not inferred from residence, occupation or an operator's website.

No additional image met both identification and reuse-rights checks. Eight existing licensed photographs remain, with the Ballogie caption clarified. No reconstructed or synthetic historical images were added.

The review is complete as a broader public-source search of this finite inventory. It is not an exhaustive reconstruction of every conveyance or inaccessible paper archive. Each page retains the specific questions that public evidence did not resolve.
